Where do drivers pass cyclists too close in Toronto?

The City counts the people who get hurt. It doesn't count the close calls that come before, or how many people ride each street. 4sight sets out to fill that gap.

Read these numbers with care. They are totals, not rates. Far more people ride downtown, so more injuries happen there. Nobody counts how many people ride each street, so these figures can't tell you whether one street is safer than another, or whether a bike lane made a difference. All lengths are approximate.

What's missing, and what 4sight adds

Injuries are rare and arrive late. Close calls happen every day, long before anyone is hurt. Decisions about bike lanes are being made without near-miss data.

A close pass is when a vehicle overtakes a rider too close, detected by a rear-facing camera on the bike.

  1. 1

    The rear camera spots the car

    A phone on the back of the bike watches the road behind and warns the rider when a vehicle comes too close.

  2. 2

    The ride uploads by itself

    Anonymously, after the ride: the route and each close pass. No account, no name, no video.

  3. 3

    It becomes a point on this map

    Together, many rides show which streets riders find stressful, and when.

Privacy

  • No account, no name, no video.
  • The app uploads the ride's route as GPS points with speed, with the first and last 50 m cut off, plus each close pass: where, when, the type of vehicle and which side it passed on.
  • This map never shows anyone's route. It shows only totals per street and close-pass dots that are not linked to any ride or person.
